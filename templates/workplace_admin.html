🛟 If someone needs more support than we can offer

You are a Workplace Support volunteer, mentor or host—not a carer. You are not expected to provide personal care, behavioural support, one-to-one supervision or clinical advice, and you are not expected to judge whether somebody needs a carer.

Before the session: open the registrations and read the Attending with / support information, access needs and what each attendee hopes to receive. Anyone attending with a carer or support worker, who is responsible for whom and their contact mobile will appear there. Keep this information private and use it only to plan the session safely.

During the session: be kind and reasonable, keep yourself and everybody else safe, and do not take on more than you are comfortable with. There should be at least two LAGC volunteers present. If somebody came with a carer or support worker, go to them first: supervision remains their responsibility. If anyone is in danger or needs urgent medical help, call 999.

Workplace conversations: offer practical peer support without promising outcomes, acting as somebody’s employer or legal representative, or giving regulated legal, financial or clinical advice. Maintain confidentiality unless a safeguarding concern requires escalation.

Afterwards: record what happened factually—what you saw, heard and did, not conclusions about the person. Chris is the charity’s safeguarding lead: email londonautismgroupcharity@gmail.com or contact him via WhatsApp so he can follow it up properly.
