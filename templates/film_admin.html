🛟 If someone needs more support than we can offer

You are a Film Club volunteer and host, not a carer. You are not expected to provide personal care, behavioural support, one-to-one support or supervision—and you are not expected to judge whether somebody needs a carer.

Before the session: open that date’s bookings and read the Attending with / support information. Anyone attending with a carer or support worker, who is responsible for whom, and their contact mobile will appear there. Make sure the lead volunteers can access those numbers before the screening starts.

In the moment: do what feels reasonable and kind, keep yourself and everybody else safe, and do not take on more than you are comfortable with. There will always be at least two volunteers, so one can remain with the group while the other helps. If somebody came with a carer or support worker, go to them first: supervision is their responsibility. If anyone is in danger or needs urgent medical help, call 999.

Afterwards: write down what actually happened as soon as you can—what you saw and did, not conclusions about the person. Chris is the charity’s safeguarding lead: email him at londonautismgroupcharity@gmail.com or contact him via WhatsApp so he can follow it up properly.

Attendees are told before booking that volunteers cannot provide personal care, behavioural support, one-to-one support or supervision. You are never obliged to fill that gap on the day.
